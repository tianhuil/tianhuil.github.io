This is a follow-up to [my earlier GPT-5.6 comparison](/blog/how-i-use-openai-gpt-5-6-based-on-175000-tests). GPT-6 has three model families in this snapshot: Luna, Sol, and Astra. Each family supports multiple reasoning-effort settings: `non-reasoning`, `low`, `medium`, `high`, `xhigh`, and `max`. That raises the practical question: should you increase effort, or move up to a more capable family?

I compared Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index results, cost per task, and time per task. The published intelligence index rises with effort across all three families.

![GPT-6 effort ladders](/blog/01-effort-vs-class-plot-01.png)

## Intelligence vs cost

Of course, intelligence is not the only metric we worry about.  Cost is a major consideration. Higher effort generally buys more Intelligence Index points, but costs more and takes longer. The biggest practical contrast is between Luna and Astra: Luna at `max` costs about $0.07 per task and takes 5.7 minutes, scoring 37. Astra at `max` costs about$3.26 and takes 7.8 minutes, scoring 53 (higher score better). Also notice that we never want to use `non-reasoning`, which is always more expensive and less intelligent than `low` for Luna and Sol.

![GPT-6 cost versus intelligence by family](/blog/01-effort-vs-class-plot-02a.png)

The analysis becomes clearer with a **Pareto frontier**, which was the analysis we chose for GPT-5.6. A model is on the intelligence-vs-cost Pareto frontier when no other model is both cheaper and more intelligent. In other words, each frontier option represents a point where you cannot improve one dimension without giving up something in the other.

![GPT-6 cost versus intelligence Pareto frontier](/blog/01-effort-vs-class-plot-02b.png)

Compared with GPT-5.6, there is a more clear tradeoff. So the new Pareto frontier has us starting with GPT-6 Luna `low` and increasing to `max` before migrating to GPT-6 Sol `med` and increasing to `max`.  This was the same pareto-optimal tradeoff we found with GPT-5.6. Put differently, GPT-5.6 Terra was simply not competitive on the pareto frontier and this may be the reason behind OpenAI not releasing Terra with GPT-6.

Finally, after GPT-6 Sol `max`, we would switch to GPT-6 Astra `medium`, increasing to `max`.  This represents a higher (and more costly) intelligence tier and is where you go for ultimate performance.

## Intelligence vs time

While cost is probably the most important metric; time is the other. Below we see the tradeoff between intelligence and time. The data did not have timing results for for `medium` reasoning-effort times, so I have not plotted them below. Again, we never want to use `non-reasoning`, which is always slower and less intelligent than `low` for Luna and Sol.

![GPT-6 time versus intelligence by family](/blog/01-effort-vs-class-plot-02c.png)

We get dramatically different results when we look at time instead of cost.  We can see that Sol `low` and Luna `xhigh` have similar intelligence performance but Luna is about 3 times cheaper but Sol is 5 times faster.  The intelligence-vs-time Pareto frontier helps better illustrate the tradeoffs.

![GPT-6 time versus intelligence Pareto frontier](/blog/01-effort-vs-class-plot-02d.png)

Following the Pareto frontier, we start at Luna `low` and move directly to Sol `low` before moving to Astra `low` and  moving up reasoning levels to Astra `max`.  Whereas we stuck to the lower families (exhausting at `max`) when we were concerned about cost, we tend to immediately jump  to the higher families when we are concerned about time.  And whereas before, the `low` reasoning efforts never made the Pareto frontier, suddenly it makes sense to use  when we care about time.

## Does effort beat moving up a family?

Across selected paired benchmark comparisons, median score gains are positive in every case:


| Comparison               | Benchmarks | Mean gain | Median gain | Bootstrap 95% CI  |
| ------------------------ | ----------: | ---------: | -----------: | -----------------: |
| Luna medium → Luna max   | 13         | +24.92    | +8.00       | \[+5.92, +60.92\] |
| Sol medium → Sol max     | 14         | +31.07    | +5.00       | \[+4.14, +80.57\] |
| Astra medium → Astra max | 14         | +10.50    | +2.50       | \[+1.64, +26.50\] |
| Luna max → Sol max       | 15         | +21.60    | +9.00       | \[+7.07, +46.60\] |
| Sol max → Astra max      | 16         | +12.31    | +6.00       | \[+4.25, +24.00\] |


The pattern is not “effort always beats a larger model.” Luna medium-to-max has a larger median gain than Astra medium-to-max, but moving Luna max to Sol max has the largest median gain of the cross-family comparisons. Mean gains are much larger than medians because a few benchmark charts have unusually large deltas.

## The simple rule

Start with Luna and raise effort until quality is sufficient. Move to Sol or Astra when the workload benefits enough to justify the much higher observed cost. Higher effort improves the published Index within each family, but every step has a cost and time tradeoff; benchmark averages cannot tell you which setting wins on your own tasks.
